          <h1 id="service-title">adversary<br>validation</h1>
          <p class="service-detail-hero__lead">Adversary validation begins with an objective, not a scanner. The work asks whether a realistic attacker can reach a protected outcome, which defensive assumptions fail along the path, and whether the organization can detect and respond before the objective is achieved.</p>
        </div>
        <aside class="service-detail-summary" aria-label="Service summary">
          <div class="service-detail-summary__row"><strong>Best for</strong><p>Organizations that need to validate controls, trust paths, detection coverage, response assumptions, or a specific high-value security objective.</p></div>
          <div class="service-detail-summary__row"><strong>Core question</strong><p>If an attacker pursues this objective under realistic constraints, what actually stops them, what detects them, and what fails?</p></div>
          <div class="service-detail-summary__row"><strong>Output</strong><p>Attack narrative, validated control gaps, supporting evidence, remediation priorities, and retest where appropriate.</p></div>
        </aside>
      </div>

  <section class="service-detail-section" aria-labelledby="adv-what-we-test">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="service-detail-intro"><h2 id="adv-what-we-test">Validate the path from initial access to business impact.</h2><p>The engagement is bounded by explicit rules of engagement and safety controls. Within those boundaries, testing follows the evidence rather than forcing the environment through a predetermined checklist.</p></div>
      <div class="service-detail-grid">
        <article class="service-detail-card"><strong>Objective &amp; Threat Path</strong><p>Define the protected outcome, likely attack paths, trust transitions, and assumptions that matter to the exercise.</p></article>
        <article class="service-detail-card"><strong>Identity &amp; Privilege</strong><p>Exercise credential, session, role, privilege, and trust relationships that can move an attacker closer to the objective.</p></article>
        <article class="service-detail-card"><strong>Control Effectiveness</strong><p>Test whether preventive controls actually block the path they are expected to block under realistic conditions.</p></article>
        <article class="service-detail-card"><strong>Detection Coverage</strong><p>Observe which attacker actions produce useful telemetry and which meaningful actions remain quiet or ambiguous.</p></article>
        <article class="service-detail-card"><strong>Response Assumptions</strong><p>Where in scope, validate whether escalation, containment, and response decisions have the information and timing they need.</p></article>
        <article class="service-detail-card"><strong>Evidence &amp; Replay</strong><p>Preserve the sequence, artifacts, and conditions needed to explain the path and reproduce important control failures.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="service-detail-section" aria-labelledby="adv-outcomes">
    <div class="wrap">
      <h2 class="svc-section-title" id="adv-outcomes">What the engagement produces</h2>
      <div class="service-detail-outcomes">
        <article class="service-outcome"><span>01</span><strong>Attack-path clarity</strong><p>A technically grounded view of which paths are viable, which are blocked, and where trust assumptions break down.</p></article>
        <article class="service-outcome"><span>02</span><strong>Control validation</strong><p>Evidence showing which controls materially change attacker behavior and which controls are weaker than assumed.</p></article>
        <article class="service-outcome"><span>03</span><strong>Detection insight</strong><p>Practical observations about what the environment exposes to defenders as the attack progresses.</p></article>
        <article class="service-outcome"><span>04</span><strong>Prioritized improvement</strong><p>Remediation framed around breaking meaningful attack paths rather than accumulating isolated findings.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

        <div class="service-detail-needs__panel"><h3>Useful inputs</h3><ul><li>The objective or defensive assumption the exercise must validate.</li><li>Authoritative scope, identities, access paths, and environment boundaries.</li><li>Known control architecture, telemetry sources, or response context where relevant.</li><li>Business impact context for the systems and outcomes being exercised.</li></ul></div>
        <div class="service-detail-needs__panel"><h3>Operational boundaries</h3><ul><li>Rules of engagement, prohibited actions, and stop conditions.</li><li>Escalation contacts and explicit authority for the agreed activity.</li><li>Traffic, availability, persistence, or data-handling constraints.</li><li>Any coordination rules for blue-team awareness or blind testing.</li></ul></div>
