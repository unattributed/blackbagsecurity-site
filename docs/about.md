        <p class="about-v8-eyebrow">Black Bag Security</p>
        <h1 id="about-title">Security engineering with memory.</h1>
        <p class="about-v8-lead">Black Bag Security is a principal-led security engineering practice for work that has to survive technical scrutiny. Offensive testing, secure systems engineering, AI security, custom tooling, evidence handling, remediation, and retest are treated as parts of the same engineering problem rather than disconnected services.</p>
      </div>

      <aside class="about-v8-hero__facts" aria-label="Black Bag Security operating profile">
        <div class="about-v8-fact"><strong>Operating model</strong><p>Direct principal ownership from scoping and test strategy through technical judgment, reporting, and retest.</p></div>
        <div class="about-v8-fact"><strong>Engineering lineage</strong><p>Hands-on computing since 1984 across mainframes, distributed systems, Unix, BSD, Linux, applications, infrastructure, offensive security, and AI-era systems.</p></div>
        <div class="about-v8-fact"><strong>Evidence standard</strong><p>Important conclusions should be reproducible, bounded by what was actually established, and useful to the people who must act on them.</p></div>
        <div class="about-v8-fact"><strong>Build when needed</strong><p>When existing tooling cannot answer the question cleanly, purpose-built validation, test, and demonstrative tooling can become part of the engagement.</p></div>
      </aside>

  <section class="about-v8-section" aria-labelledby="why-bbs">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="about-v8-section__head">
        <div><p class="about-v8-eyebrow">Why Black Bag Security</p><h2 id="why-bbs">The answer matters more than the activity.</h2></div>
        <p>Security work is easy to make busy and difficult to make decisive. A scanner can produce output. A framework can produce coverage. Neither automatically answers whether an important attack path is real, what it means to the organization, what caused it, or whether the correction actually worked. Black Bag Security exists for the work between those points.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-v8-three">
        <article class="about-v8-card"><span>01 / UNDERSTAND</span><h3>Find the security condition that matters</h3><p>Start with the system, the business concern, the attacker model, and the decision the engagement needs to support. Test activity is selected because it can change that decision.</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-card"><span>02 / ENGINEER</span><h3>Build when the problem demands it</h3><p>Use established tools where they are sufficient. When they are not, engineer the validator, harness, protocol workflow, controlled exploit demonstration, or PoC needed to make the condition observable and repeatable.</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-card"><span>03 / CLOSE</span><h3>Carry the work through remediation</h3><p>A useful engagement does not stop at detection. Findings should connect to root cause, practical remediation, residual risk, and a defensible way to verify the security outcome.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>

        <p class="about-v8-eyebrow">Engineering lineage</p>
        <h2 id="lineage-title">Built across generations of computing.</h2>
        <p>The value of the timeline is not nostalgia. It is context. Systems fail differently, but many of the underlying lessons persist: boundaries matter, state matters, assumptions matter, operational reality matters, and abstractions eventually meet implementation.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-v8-lineage__rail">
        <article class="about-v8-era"><strong>1984+</strong><h3>Computing from the inside out</h3><p>Operating systems, programming languages, applications, integration, and the practical consequences of how systems are actually assembled.</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-era"><strong>Early 1990s</strong><h3>Mainframes meet distributed systems</h3><p>Experience expands across mainframe and networked environments, Unix, BSD, and the operational differences between centralized and distributed computing.</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-era"><strong>1993-1996</strong><h3>Linux and OpenBSD enter the toolkit</h3><p>Linux becomes part of the working environment in 1993. OpenBSD follows in 1996, reinforcing an engineering preference for transparency, least privilege, and controlled system behavior.</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-era"><strong>2000s-2010s</strong><h3>Offensive security becomes an engineering discipline</h3><p>Web applications, identity, networks, infrastructure, secure systems, and adversarial testing converge around a common question: what can actually fail under pressure?</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-era"><strong>2020+</strong><h3>AI changes the execution model</h3><p>Browser-integrated AI, agentic systems, local models, governed automation, and evidence-aware tooling introduce new trust boundaries without removing the old ones.</p></article>

          <h2 id="principal-title">Duncan Stirling</h2>
          <p>Black Bag Security is led through direct technical ownership. The person responsible for the security judgment remains close to the work that creates it: scoping, test strategy, manual investigation, purpose-built tooling, evidence review, finding decisions, reporting, remediation discussion, and retest.</p>
          <p>This model deliberately reduces the distance between the customer, the operator performing the work, and the person accountable for the conclusion. It is designed for engagements where context, technical judgment, confidentiality, and continuity matter more than throughput.</p>

          <div class="about-v8-ownership">
            <div><strong>Scope and strategy</strong><span>Objectives, authority, constraints, attacker model, and test design remain connected.</span></div>
            <div><strong>Technical judgment</strong><span>Scanner output, automation, and AI assistance do not become findings without evidence and review.</span></div>
            <div><strong>Closure</strong><span>Reporting, remediation context, and retest preserve the reasoning that produced the original conclusion.</span></div>

  <section class="about-v8-section" aria-labelledby="standards-title">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="about-v8-section__head">
        <div><p class="about-v8-eyebrow">Operating standards</p><h2 id="standards-title">The work should remain defensible after the engagement ends.</h2></div>
        <p>Black Bag Security is organized around repeatability, explicit authority, secure handling, evidence integrity, and transparent limitations. The goal is not to make every engagement look the same. It is to make the important decisions and artifacts understandable enough that another qualified person can review how the conclusion was reached.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-v8-three">
        <article class="about-v8-card"><span>AUTHORITY</span><h3>Controlled execution</h3><p>Scope, access, prohibited actions, stop conditions, escalation paths, and data-handling requirements are treated as inputs to the technical system.</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-card"><span>EVIDENCE</span><h3>Reproducible conclusions</h3><p>Material claims are connected to proportionate evidence and the conditions under which the behavior was established. Limitations remain visible rather than being edited away.</p></article>
        <article class="about-v8-card"><span>HANDLING</span><h3>Confidential by design</h3><p>Sensitive access, client data, screenshots, captures, credentials, working artifacts, and delivery paths are minimized and handled according to the engagement requirements.</p></article>
