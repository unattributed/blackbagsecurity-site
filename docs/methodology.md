        <article class="method-promise-card"><span class="method-promise-card__number">01</span><span class="icon-mask icon-target" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Purpose before tools</h3><p>Start with the security question and the decision the engagement needs to support.   <section class="method-v2-lifecycle" id="operating-model" aria-labelledby="method-lifecycle">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="method-v2-lifecycle__head">
        <div><p class="method-eyebrow">Operating model</p><h2 id="method-lifecycle">From security question to verified outcome.</h2></div>
        <p>Principal judgment establishes the objective and authority. Black Bag tooling accelerates execution and maintains evidence-backed engagement state. Evidence, not activity, determines the conclusion.</p>
      </div>

      <div class="method-v2-lifecycle__grid">
        <article class="method-step"><span>01 / FRAME</span><h3>Define the decision</h3><p>Identify the system, concern, business context, important workflows, and what the assessment must establish.</p></article>
        <article class="method-step"><span>02 / AUTHORIZE</span><h3>Bound the engagement</h3><p>Agree scope, access, permitted actions, safety limits, escalation, data handling, prerequisites, and stop conditions.</p></article>
        <article class="method-step"><span>03 / EXECUTE</span><h3>Test and adapt</h3><p>Progress through applicable objectives using principal analysis, AOTP, and purpose-fit tooling. Troubleshoot test-specific failures and create reusable test logic where it improves coverage.</p></article>
        <article class="method-step"><span>04 / VALIDATE</span><h3>Challenge the result</h3><p>Reproduce important behavior, compare controls, eliminate false positives, establish conditions and impact, and preserve supporting evidence.</p></article>
        <article class="method-step"><span>05 / RECONCILE</span><h3>Keep state usable</h3><p>Maintain coverage, findings, limitations, evidence, communications, and delivery state so reporting reflects what the assessment has actually established.</p></article>
        <article class="method-step"><span>06 / VERIFY</span><h3>Test the correction</h3><p>Where retest is in scope, return to the original failure path and document whether the issue is resolved, mitigated, unchanged, or unable to be verified.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="method-tooling" id="black-bag-tooling" aria-labelledby="tooling-title">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="method-tooling__intro">
        <div>
          <p class="method-eyebrow">Black Bag engineering</p>
          <h2 id="tooling-title">AI assistance without surrendering the engagement.</h2>
        </div>
        <div class="method-tooling__intro-copy">
          <p>AOTP and the Security Engagement Platform are internal Black Bag systems used to carry the mechanics of a modern security assessment without outsourcing accountability. AOTP supports local AI-assisted planning, governed testing, evidence capture, replay, and validation. The Security Engagement Platform keeps authorization, coverage, evidence, communications, delivery, and retest state traceable throughout the engagement.</p>
          <p>The architecture is intentional. AOTP uses local model planning and governed local tools. Sensitive engagement state is segregated from reusable source, and client delivery is sanitized from internal preservation. The objective is to gain the depth and consistency of AI-assisted testing without creating unnecessary third-party exposure of client intellectual property, confidential data, credentials, or evidence.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="method-tooling__loop" aria-label="Engagement control loop">
        <span>OBJECTIVE</span><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><span>TEST</span><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><span>EVIDENCE</span><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><span>RECONCILE</span><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><span>COMMUNICATE</span><i aria-hidden="true">→</i><span>NEXT OBJECTIVE</span>
      </div>

      <div class="method-tooling__grid">
        <article><span>01</span><h3>Plan and sequence</h3><p>Choose the next applicable objective from scope, current coverage, dependencies, and existing evidence rather than following a static script blindly.</p></article>
        <article><span>02</span><h3>Execute with AOTP</h3><p>Use local AI-assisted planning, governed tools, controlled testing, replay, and validation to deepen coverage while keeping the principal in control of the engagement.</p></article>
        <article><span>03</span><h3>Preserve evidence</h3><p>Capture the minimum evidence needed to support the actual security property, maintain provenance, and keep live secrets and unnecessary client data out of tracked artifacts.</p></article>
        <article><span>04</span><h3>Maintain engagement state</h3><p>The Security Engagement Platform reconciles coverage, evidence references, findings, limitations, and test disposition so stale interim conclusions do not become final assessment state.</p></article>
        <article><span>05</span><h3>Communicate and report</h3><p>Generate concise status, blockers, coverage, evidence references, and reporting inputs from the reconciled engagement state instead of reconstructing the story at the end.</p></article>
      </div>

      <aside class="method-tooling__control" aria-label="Principal control plane">
        <div class="method-tooling__control-label">Principal control plane</div>
        <div><strong>Scope and authority</strong><p>What may be tested, where, when, and under which restrictions.</p></div>
        <div><strong>Material judgment</strong><p>Whether evidence supports a finding, its significance, and the language used to describe it.</p></div>
        <div><strong>Confidentiality</strong><p>How intellectual property, credentials, client data, evidence, and working material are handled and disclosed.</p></div>
        <div><strong>External accountability</strong><p>The principal remains responsible for client communication, conclusions, report quality, and delivery.</p></div>
      </aside>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="method-v2-evidence" id="evidence-first" aria-labelledby="evidence-title">
    <div class="wrap method-v2-evidence__grid">
      <div class="method-v2-evidence__statement">
        <p class="method-eyebrow">Evidence-first</p>
        <h2 id="evidence-title">A lead is not automatically a finding.</h2>
        <p>A scanner alert, automated signal, unusual response, suspicious state change, or promising attack path starts an investigation. Material conclusions are stronger when the behavior can be reproduced, compared against controls, placed in context, and connected to real impact.</p>
        <blockquote>Automation can increase the amount of work performed. It does not lower the standard of proof.</blockquote>
      </div>

      <div class="method-v2-evidence__qualities">
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-evidence" aria-hidden="true"></span><div><strong>Reproducible</strong><p>Capture enough context, conditions, requests, responses, state, or artifacts for important behavior to be understood and repeated where practical.</p></div></article>
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-doc" aria-hidden="true"></span><div><strong>Traceable</strong><p>Connect conclusions to the applicable test objective, target, conditions, evidence, disposition, finding, and report state.</p></div></article>
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-shield" aria-hidden="true"></span><div><strong>Integrity checked</strong><p>Use manifests, cryptographic hashes, controlled transfer, and signed preservation or delivery artifacts where the engagement warrants it.</p></div></article>
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-data" aria-hidden="true"></span><div><strong>Minimized and protected</strong><p>Preserve what is required to support the conclusion while keeping credentials, live sessions, unnecessary client data, and internal working material out of client delivery.</p></div></article>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="method-v2-standards" aria-labelledby="standards-title">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="method-v2-intro method-v2-intro--standards">
        <div><p class="method-eyebrow">Coverage and judgment</p><h2 id="standards-title">Standards give structure. Judgment gives meaning.</h2></div>
        <p>Established testing standards organize coverage and make work easier to review. They remain guides, not substitutes for understanding the application, architecture, business logic, actual attack surface, and evidence.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="method-v2-standards__grid">
        <article><span>01</span><h3>Use frameworks where they fit</h3><p>For application work, references such as OWASP WSTG and ASVS can structure coverage, verification, and control mapping.</p></article>
        <article><span>02</span><h3>Follow the system where it leads</h3><p>Architecture, roles, workflows, data, constraints, and observed behavior determine where deeper testing is warranted.</p></article>
        <article><span>03</span><h3>Separate coverage from conclusions</h3><p>A completed test is not automatically a vulnerability. A blocked prerequisite is not a passed control. Not Applicable, Blocked, Completed, Finding, and unresolved states remain distinct.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="method-v2-communication" aria-labelledby="communication-title">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="method-v2-communication__head">
        <p class="method-eyebrow">Continuous communication</p>
        <h2 id="communication-title">No disappearing into a black box.</h2>
        <p>Client communication is generated from the current evidence-backed engagement state. That keeps progress, blockers, limitations, findings, next work, and delivery status consistent with what the testing has actually established.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="method-v2-communication__grid">
        <article><strong>Before testing</strong><p>Confirm objectives, scope, access, responsibilities, constraints, prerequisites, testing cadence, and escalation paths.</p></article>
        <article><strong>During testing</strong><p>Surface meaningful progress, blockers, scope questions, prerequisite gaps, material observations, and the next planned objectives without exposing unnecessary internal working detail.</p></article>
        <article><strong>At closeout</strong><p>Separate confirmed findings, expected controls, limitations, blocked work, unresolved questions, residual risk, evidence state, and retest status.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="method-v2-delivery" aria-labelledby="delivery-title">
    <div class="wrap">
      <div class="method-v2-intro">
        <div><p class="method-eyebrow">Secure delivery</p><h2 id="delivery-title">Protect the engagement as carefully as the target.</h2></div>
        <p>Testing requires privileged access and creates sensitive evidence. The methodology protects that material from collection through transfer, preservation, sanitization, reporting, and final delivery.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="method-v2-delivery__grid">
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-target" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Explicit authority</h3><p>Work stays inside the agreed scope and rules of engagement. Ambiguity is escalated before activity expands.</p></article>
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-evidence" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Evidence provenance</h3><p>Important artifacts can be manifest-driven, hash-verified, and preserved independently from the working environment.</p></article>
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-data" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Sanitized delivery</h3><p>Client-facing evidence is separated from internal preservation so credentials, session material, unnecessary data, and private working state are not delivered accidentally.</p></article>
        <article><span class="icon-mask icon-shield" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Integrity verification</h3><p>Where appropriate, transfers and delivery artifacts are checked cryptographically so what was collected, analyzed, and delivered can be reconciled.</p></article>

  <section class="method-v2-retest" id="remediation-and-retest" aria-labelledby="retest-title">
    <div class="wrap method-v2-retest__grid">
      <div>
        <p class="method-eyebrow">Remediation and retest</p>
        <h2 id="retest-title">A code change is not the same as a proven fix.</h2>
        <p>Where verification is part of the engagement, the retest returns to the original failure path. The question is not whether something changed. The question is whether the security condition that created the finding still exists.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="method-v2-retest__states">
        <article><strong>Resolved</strong><p>The original failure path can no longer be reproduced under the retest conditions.</p></article>
        <article><strong>Mitigated</strong><p>Risk is reduced, but the underlying condition or a meaningful variant remains.</p></article>
        <article><strong>Not resolved</strong><p>The original issue or equivalent security impact remains reproducible.</p></article>
        <article><strong>Unable to verify</strong><p>A missing prerequisite, unavailable environment, or other constraint prevents a defensible retest conclusion.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
