  <section class="iv3-section iv3-section--white" id="principles"><div class="wrap"><div class="iv3-section-head"><div><p class="iv3-eyebrow">Operating principles</p><h2>Four promises shape the work.</h2></div><p>The test plan changes with the system, risk, and constraints. These principles do not.</p></div><div class="iv3-glance"><article><span>01 / Purpose</span><strong>Purpose before tools</strong><p>Start with the security question, not the toolset.</p></article><article><span>02 / Safety</span><strong>Safety before speed</strong><p>Authority, stop conditions, and escalation are explicit.</p></article><article><span>03 / Evidence</span><strong>Evidence before claims</strong><p>Important conclusions must survive scrutiny.</p></article><article><span>04 / Closure</span><strong>Closure before completion</strong><p>The work should lead to action and a way to verify it.</p></article></div></div></section>

  <section class="iv3-section iv3-section--dark" id="operating-model" aria-labelledby="model-title"><div class="wrap"><div class="iv3-section-head"><div><p class="iv3-eyebrow">Operating model</p><h2 id="model-title">From security question to verified outcome.</h2></div><p>Each phase removes a different kind of uncertainty: what matters, what is authorized, what can fail, what can be proven, what should change, and whether the change worked.</p></div><div class="iv3-process"><article><span class="iv3-process__dot">01</span><div><h3>Understand</h3><p>Identify the system, concern, and decision.</p></div></article><article><span class="iv3-process__dot">02</span><div><h3>Authorize</h3><p>Bound scope, access, safety, and handling.</p></div></article><article><span class="iv3-process__dot">03</span><div><h3>Test</h3><p>Exercise realistic failure paths.</p></div></article><article><span class="iv3-process__dot">04</span><div><h3>Validate</h3><p>Reproduce, challenge, and preserve evidence.</p></div></article><article><span class="iv3-process__dot">05</span><div><h3>Report &amp; verify</h3><p>Make the answer usable and test the correction.</p></div></article></div></div></section>

  <section class="iv3-section" id="evidence-first" aria-labelledby="evidence-title"><div class="wrap"><div class="iv3-section-head"><div><p class="iv3-eyebrow">Evidence-first</p><h2 id="evidence-title">A lead is not automatically a finding.</h2></div><p>A scanner alert, unusual response, suspicious state change, or promising attack path starts an investigation. Material conclusions become stronger when behavior can be reproduced, placed in context, and connected to real impact.</p></div><div class="iv3-card-grid"><article class="iv3-card"><span class="iv3-card__index">01 / REPRODUCIBLE</span><h3>Preserve enough context to repeat what matters.</h3><p>Requests, responses, state, conditions, artifacts, identities, and timing are captured in proportion to the conclusion.</p></article><article class="iv3-card"><span class="iv3-card__index">02 / TRACEABLE</span><h3>Connect the claim to its evidence and limits.</h3><p>Important conclusions should show what was observed, what was inferred, what was not established, and how the result was classified.</p></article><article class="iv3-card"><span class="iv3-card__index">03 / PROPORTIONATE</span><h3>Collect what proves the point, not everything available.</h3><p>Evidence minimization reduces exposure while keeping conclusions defensible.</p></article><article class="iv3-card"><span class="iv3-card__index">04 / PROTECTED</span><h3>Treat engagement artifacts as security-sensitive material.</h3><p>Credentials, client data, screenshots, logs, captures, and working evidence are handled accordingly.</p></article></div><div class="iv3-quote"><div class="iv3-quote__mark">“</div><div><blockquote>We would rather state an honest limitation than manufacture certainty the evidence does not support.</blockquote><p>Bounded confidence is part of the methodology.</p></div></div></div></section>

  <section class="iv3-section iv3-section--soft" id="delivery"><div class="wrap"><div class="iv3-section-head"><div><p class="iv3-eyebrow">Secure delivery</p><h2>Protect the engagement as carefully as the target.</h2></div><p>Security testing often requires privileged access and creates sensitive evidence. Those materials should not become a new source of risk.</p></div><div class="iv3-audience"><article><span>Authority</span><h3>Explicit scope</h3><p>Ambiguity is resolved before activity expands.</p></article><article><span>Data</span><h3>Minimal collection</h3><p>Use synthetic, disposable, or limited data where practical.</p></article><article><span>Operations</span><h3>Stop and escalate</h3><p>Unexpected production risk or unclear authority triggers escalation.</p></article></div><div class="iv3-deep"><details><summary>Coverage, standards, and judgment</summary><div>Established references such as OWASP WSTG and ASVS can structure coverage and verification, but they do not replace understanding the actual architecture, roles, workflows, data, and trust decisions.</div></details><details><summary>Communication during the engagement</summary><div>Before testing, confirm objectives, access, responsibilities, constraints, and escalation. During testing, surface meaningful progress, blockers, scope questions, and material findings. At closeout, separate confirmed findings, limitations, unresolved questions, residual risk, and retest status.</div></details><details><summary>Evidence handling</summary><div>Evidence is kept proportionate, traceable, protected, and aligned to the engagement's handling requirements. Sensitive materials are not collected merely for completeness.</div></details></div></div></section>

  <section class="iv3-section iv3-section--white" id="remediation-and-retest"><div class="wrap"><div class="iv3-section-head"><div><p class="iv3-eyebrow">Remediation &amp; retest</p><h2>A code change is not the same as a proven fix.</h2></div><p>Where verification is part of the engagement, retesting returns to the original failure path. The question is whether the security condition still exists.</p></div><div class="iv3-glance"><article><span>Resolved</span><strong>Failure path no longer reproduces.</strong><p>The original condition cannot be reproduced under retest conditions.</p></article><article><span>Mitigated</span><strong>Risk reduced, condition remains.</strong><p>The change narrows impact but does not fully remove the security condition.</p></article><article><span>Not resolved</span><strong>Issue remains reproducible.</strong><p>The original or equivalent security impact is still present.</p></article><article><span>Unable to verify</span><strong>No defensible retest conclusion.</strong><p>A missing prerequisite or unavailable environment prevents verification.</p></article></div></div></section>

  <section class="iv3-section" id="reporting"><div class="wrap"><div class="iv3-section-head"><div><p class="iv3-eyebrow">Reporting</p><h2>One body of evidence. Different readers.</h2></div><p>The report should work for the person making the risk decision and the person responsible for fixing the problem.</p></div><div class="iv3-audience"><article><span>Leadership</span><h3>What matters and what happens next</h3><p>Risk context, material findings, limitations, priorities, and decisions that require attention.</p></article><article><span>Engineering &amp; Security</span><h3>Enough detail to reproduce and remediate</h3><p>Technical evidence, affected behavior, root cause, conditions, remediation direction, and retest context.</p></article><article><span>Governance &amp; Assurance</span><h3>What was covered and established</h3><p>Scope, methodology, limitations, finding status, evidence-backed conclusions, and closure state.</p></article></div></div></section>
